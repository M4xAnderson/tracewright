# The second explorer composes the open nets itself and checks every piece of evidence against its own composition

Amends ADR 0008. The second explorer in `recheck` never sees the net that `engine` flattened. It receives the unflattened `OpenNets` (the sibling nets, their fusions and their asynchronous links) and composes them itself with its own interpretation of the rules below. Before anything else it compares the canonical encoding of its composition with the engine's flattened net; a difference ends the check as Unknown "internal disagreement", whatever the property. Exhaustive proofs, certificates (P-invariants, later ILP) and counterexamples are then all checked against `recheck`'s own net, so a flattening bug cannot be confirmed by code that shares it. The cross-check covers composition only: lowering from the Model to `OpenNets` (port binding, and from slice 2 the compilation of state machines) is shared by both explorers and is covered by golden tests per binding rule and, for state machines, by a model-level replay of every counterexample in `engine`. The second explorer runs after the main one, never in parallel; the main explorer may use at most half of the budget's markings and time, drops its visited set, and the rest goes to `recheck`. A check whose re-check runs out of budget is Unknown "budget exhausted while re-checking", never Proved. The worker computes the CheckKey from the net it actually explored. Disproved needs evidence by property kind: a property whose violation is a counterexample (deadlock-free, no capacity exceeded, never p) is confirmed by replaying it on `recheck`'s net, where every step must be enabled, no step may start from an overflow marking, and the last marking must violate the property; a property whose violation has no finite counterexample (no dead transitions: the transition is enabled in no reachable marking) is confirmed exactly like a Proved by exhaustion, by agreement of `recheck`'s exploration; always reachable p is confirmed by replaying the counterexample and then by agreement that no p-marking is reachable from its last marking. Running out of budget during any of these gives Unknown, never Disproved.

## Semantics (normative for both explorers)

- Qualified IDs: own place or transition `element/name`; fused transition `relationship/caller-transition+callee-transition`; generated place `relationship`. Markings, properties, counterexamples and encodings use these IDs, never positions.
- Firing: a transition is enabled iff every pre place holds at least the arc weight and every post place with `block` stays within its capacity after firing. Firing removes and adds the arc weights; `drop` keeps at most the capacity; `overflow` may exceed it and yields an overflow marking (ADR 0014).
- Sync binding: one fused transition per (relationship, caller transition, callee transition) with the union of both pre and post sets; a bound port transition does not fire on its own. Async binding: a generated place between the two port transitions with the relationship's capacity and `when_full`.
- Scope: in an element's own scope its ports are closed (a transition port fires alone, a place port is a plain place). In a combined scope every port of every child must be bound and every child must have a behavior model; otherwise lowering fails with "model incomplete" and the property is Unknown. A relationship that binds no ports contributes nothing and is listed as an assumption.
- Deadlock: no transition enabled and the marking is not final. Final markings are a predicate over the combined marking (slice 4; before that there are none). Overflow markings are neither deadlocks nor final and are not expanded.
- No dead transitions: Disproved with the set of transitions enabled in no reachable marking. Never p: Disproved by a counterexample whose last marking satisfies p. Always reachable p: from every reachable marking some p-marking is reachable; Disproved by a counterexample to a marking from which none is.

## Consequences

- A Proved by exhaustion is limited by what a plain breadth-first search can explore within half the budget; once `verify` gains reductions, `recheck`'s search also supplies the shortest counterexample for display.
- Every rule above has a golden test in E1 that both crates must pass.
